About EatAware

We Decode What India Eats

EatAware is India's first Ingredient Intelligence Platform — built so that every Indian consumer can understand exactly what is inside their food and what it does to their body, today and over time.

Why We Built This

India has over 1.4 billion consumers who pick up packaged food every day — biscuits, noodles, chips, health drinks — and read nothing on the label beyond the brand name and price.

Yet the ingredients list on the back of every product tells the complete story — which oils were used, which preservatives were added, which dyes colour the product, and what long-term effects daily consumption creates in the body.

EatAware exists to translate that ingredient list from chemical jargon into plain, honest English backed by evidence — and to give every product a single honest grade that tells you where it stands.

Our Methodology

How We Grade Every Product A to F

Our grading system is an EatAware editorial model built on FSSAI label data, WHO nutritional guidance, and peer-reviewed public-health evidence — calibrated specifically for Indian dietary patterns and FSSAI food categories. Singapore's Nutri-Grade beverage thresholds are used as one reference anchor for beverage grading only.

1
We read the back of the product label
Every rating starts with the ingredients list on the back of the actual physical product — not marketing claims on the front. We look at the full ingredient declaration as mandated by FSSAI for all packaged foods sold in India. Ingredients are listed in descending order of weight — so whatever appears first is present in the highest quantity.
2
We identify every ingredient's health impact
Each ingredient is cross-referenced against WHO classifications, IARC carcinogen listings, FSSAI permitted additive lists, and peer-reviewed nutritional research. We classify each ingredient as Safe (green), Caution (amber) or Avoid (red). Chemical names are translated — TBHQ becomes "Synthetic additive Preservative", Tartrazine becomes "Synthetic Yellow Colour (INS 102)" — so the risk is immediately understood.
3
We assess nutritional quality — sugar, sodium, fat, fibre
Beyond additives, we evaluate the nutritional composition: total sugar per serving, sodium content, type of fat (saturated, trans, or healthy), and fibre content. High sodium above 600mg per serving, added sugar above 10g per serving, or trans fats in any quantity all negatively impact the grade. Fibre above 3g, protein above 5g, or natural whole ingredients improve it.
4
We score long-term body impact — not just today
A product eaten once is different from a product eaten daily for 10 years. Our body impact text specifically addresses what daily or weekly consumption does to the body over 1, 5 and 10 years — based on clinical longitudinal studies. A D-grade product eaten monthly has different implications than a D-grade product in a child's lunchbox every day.
5
We assign a single honest A–F grade
All factors combine into one grade. No product can buy a better grade — no brand has ever paid EatAware, and none ever will. The grade is our honest assessment of what this product does to an Indian body that consumes it regularly. Grades are reviewed and updated when formulations change.

What Each Grade Means

A
Excellent — Whole food, clean ingredients, no harmful additives. Safe for daily consumption by all ages.
B
Good — Minor concerns but safe for daily consumption. Small improvements would move it to A.
C
Moderate — Acceptable occasionally. Not for daily consumption by children. Better options exist.
D
High Concern — Harmful additives or very high sugar/sodium. Monthly consumption for adults only. Avoid for children.
E
Very Poor — Significant health risk from additives or nutrition profile. Once a month maximum for adults.
F
Avoid — Very high concern for frequent use. Contains ingredients associated with higher long-term risk when consumed frequently. Best avoided for daily use.
